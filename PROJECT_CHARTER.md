# Project Charter

## Purpose

Create a rigorous, understandable, and reproducible data analytics project that satisfies the course requirements and can be confidently presented by the entire group.

## Current decision

We are **collecting and comparing use cases**. The research topic, data, methods, and final deliverables have not yet been chosen. This is intentional: we will test feasibility before committing.

## Desired project characteristics

The selected idea should:

- answer one clear question;
- have enough lawful, accessible, and understandable data;
- allow a complete analysis before 28 October 2026;
- include a meaningful baseline and measurable outcome;
- be explainable to an introductory data analytics audience;
- divide into useful contributions without isolating knowledge in one teammate;
- produce a strong visual and written story; and
- remain valuable even if the result is negative or inconclusive.

## Scope

### In scope

- use-case proposals and selection;
- data-source feasibility checks;
- data collection, documentation, cleaning, and analysis;
- statistical or machine-learning methods justified by the question;
- reproducible notebooks and reusable code;
- micropublication, figures, presentation, and Q&A preparation.

### Out of scope until the team approves it

- collecting sensitive personal data;
- paying for data or services;
- using a source whose licence does not permit our use;
- committing secrets, credentials, or large raw datasets to GitHub;
- deploying a public application;
- adding analysis simply because it looks sophisticated.

## Definition of done

The project is complete when the course checklist passes, the final results can be reproduced from documented steps, all headline claims point to evidence, and every teammate can explain the research question, data, method, result, and limitations.

## Roles

Roles will be assigned after topic selection because the work depends on the chosen data and method. Regardless of ownership, everyone reviews the final research question, evaluation, conclusions, and presentation.
